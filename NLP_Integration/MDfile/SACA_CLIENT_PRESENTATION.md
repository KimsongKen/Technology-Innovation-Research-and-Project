# SACA — Client Presentation & Pitch Guide

**Audience:** Clinic managers, healthcare administrators, and government stakeholders focused on patient outcomes, safety, and workflow.

**Purpose:** Executive summary and pitch narrative—no implementation detail. Use this as slide copy and speaker cues.

---

## Slide 1 — The SACA Vision *(Executive Summary)*

### On screen

**Hook**

SACA is a **smart, bilingual clinical assistant** designed to **empower remote health workers**, **reduce language barriers**, and ensure **no critical patient falls through the cracks**.

**Core value**

It acts as a **digital safety net**, instantly translating what patients describe into **clear, actionable medical triage**—so the right level of care is visible **fast**, and decisions stay **grounded in safety**.

### Pitch cues *(say this way)*

- Lead with outcomes: **fewer missed red flags**, **fairer access for patients who don’t speak English at home**, **less cognitive load on stretched nurses**.
- Position SACA as support for people already doing hard work in the field—not a substitute for clinical judgment.

## Slide 2 — How It Works *(The 4-Step Patient Journey)*

*This slide turns “how data moves through the system” into a simple story anyone can follow.*

### On screen — The journey

**1 — Listen**

The patient speaks their symptoms **in English or Warlpiri** into the tablet. The system captures what was said so nothing depends on memory alone.

**2 — Verify *(Human-in-the-Loop)***

The nurse sees the text and can **instantly edit or confirm** it. **AI supports the nurse; it does not replace them.** What the nurse confirms is what drives the next step—**reliability** starts with **human authority**.

**3 — Analyze**

Behind the scenes, a **secure clinical engine** compares the verified symptoms against a large, structured body of **medical reference data**—**quickly** and **consistently**—to estimate how urgent the situation may be.

**4 — Act**

The app delivers a **clear triage level**—**Severe**, **Moderate**, or **Mild**—plus a **recommended action plan** in language staff can use **immediately** at the bedside or in telehealth.

### Pitch cues

- Emphasize **speed** where it matters: **faster situational clarity** for the team on shift.
- Repeat the line: **“The nurse is always in the loop.”** That builds trust with clinical and regulatory audiences.

## Slide 3 — Breaking Language Barriers *(The Warlpiri Breakthrough)*

*This slide translates the “bilingual pathway” into plain language—no speech-science or model names.*

### On screen

**Why it matters**

In remote communities, **language should never be a barrier to safe care.** Patients deserve to describe how they feel **in the language they trust**.

**What SACA does**

SACA is built to **natively understand Warlpiri** in the listening step: it hears **Indigenous language**, carries the **clinical meaning** into **English** for the verification and analysis steps, and applies the **same safety-minded triage** as for English speakers.

**What clients should remember**

- **Accuracy** for Warlpiri speakers is treated as a **clinical equity** issue—not an optional add-on.
- The design keeps **one controlled medical vocabulary** after translation so **outcomes stay comparable and auditable** across languages.

### Pitch cues

- Pair this slide with a real workflow image: **patient → tablet → nurse review → triage card**.
- If asked “how good is the recognition?”—answer in **patient terms**: *we aim for what the patient said to be reflected in what the nurse confirms*, and we **never bypass** the nurse verification step.

## Slide 4 — Patient Safety Above All Else *(The Safety Net)*

*This is the most important slide for clients. It maps directly to “clinical escalation gates”—as policy, not guesswork.*

### On screen — Non-negotiable rules

SACA is built with **strict, unbreakable medical rules** layered **on top of** any estimated risk. **Safety overrides convenience—always.**

**Example 1 — When the system is unsure**

If the engine is **not confident enough** in a Mild or Moderate result, SACA **does not quietly downplay risk.** It **automatically escalates** the patient to **Severe** so a **doctor reviews them without delay**. **We err on the side of protecting the patient.**

**Example 2 — When words signal an emergency**

If the encounter includes **unmistakable crisis language**—for example **“chest pain”** or **“can’t breathe”** (and similar high-acuity phrases the product encodes)—the app **does not rely on the estimate alone.** It **forces Severe** and triggers an **emergency-appropriate alert path**. **We never take chances with patient lives.**

**Why stakeholders care**

- These overrides are **explicit, predefined, and logged**—they are **governance**, not hidden automatism.
- The design reflects a simple ethic: **expected harm from missing a serious case outweighs the inconvenience of extra caution.**

### Pitch cues

- For administrators: tie to **risk management**—**defensible escalation** and **clear audit story**.
- For clinicians: stress **deterministic red-flag handling** so **plain language emergencies** cannot be “talked down” by an imperfect estimate.

## Slide 5 — Transparent and Auditable *(No Black Boxes)*

*This slide covers “dual provenance” and “explainability” without using those terms.*

### On screen — We show our work

**Top reasons for the recommendation**

SACA returns the **top three symptoms** that most strongly drove the triage suggestion. The nurse sees **why** the system landed on a given level—supporting **shared understanding** between **human and assistant**, not blind trust.

**Two records, one clear story**

Every interaction preserves **two intentional traces**:

- **What listening captured first** (the automated best effort from speech)—for **quality review** and continuous improvement.
- **What the nurse attested as correct**—the **authoritative** version used for the clinical step.

That separation protects **integrity**: if something was misheard, the record shows **both** the raw capture and the **human-corrected** text—**critical for legal, quality, and governance review.**

**Logging for accountability**

Outcomes note when **safety rules** changed the level (for example, from unsure to Severe, or from a keyword override). **Transparency** and **traceability** are built in—not bolted on after the fact.

### Pitch cues

- **Reliability** here means: *you can explain the case later*—to a peer, a coroner’s inquiry, or a funding partner.
- Avoid promising “perfect AI.” Promise **structured transparency**, **nurse sovereignty over the charted text**, and **safety-first defaults**.
